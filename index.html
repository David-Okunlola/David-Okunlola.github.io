About

From research question to credible evidence

I am a quantitative social scientist and PhD candidate in Demography at Florida State University, with 15+ peer-reviewed publications and extensive experience analyzing large, complex survey and population-health datasets.

I specialize in applied quantitative research using Stata and R, with experience in complex survey analysis, survey-weighted estimation, multilevel modeling, multiple imputation, structural equation modeling, longitudinal and observational data analysis, and causal inference methods.

My research spans population health, health disparities, reproductive and maternal health, gender-based and intimate partner violence, injury prevention, migration, and social inequality. I am comfortable taking projects from research question and study design through data preparation, statistical analysis, interpretation, and dissemination.

What I bring to a team is the ability to turn complex data into credible, decision-relevant evidence. I can develop analytical strategies, clean and manage large datasets, apply appropriate statistical methods, account for survey design and missing data, produce reproducible analyses, and communicate findings clearly to technical and non-technical audiences.

Firearm access and secured storage among older men in the United States

Uses the Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System to examine which older men have access to firearms at home and whether those firearms are stored securely. The project draws on life course and precarious manhood perspectives, uses DAG-based causal modeling, and links individual survey responses to state policy and political context.

Recent work

New findings, presented at SDA

Nigeria · 2023–24 DHS · women aged 15–49

Are victims of sexual violence more likely to experience technology-facilitated gender-based violence, or is it the other way around?

Southern Demographic Association · with O. A. Makinde, D. Lucy, B. O. Muheeb, M.-E. Bolorunduro, O. A. Jegede

One of the first studies of what predicts technology-facilitated gender-based violence (TFGBV) in Nigeria, and whether it travels with sexual violence. Framed by lifestyle and routine activities theory.

Code: DaveIpsos/TFGBV

What we found

1.6%prevalenceof women reported lifetime TFGBV, such as threatening messages or non-consensual sharing of sexual images.
+9 pp95% CI 3–15higher probability of recent TFGBV among women who had ever experienced sexual violence, after entropy balancing.
↔Instrumental-variable models suggest the association runs in both directions between sexual violence and TFGBV.

Implication: services for sexual violence and digital-safety policy need to be designed together.

United States · 2023 NHIS · foreign-born adults

Race-ethnic differences in perceived discrimination and vigilance among foreign-born adults

Southern Demographic Association · with T. Asitibasi, T. Agoben, O. Oluwasanmi, O. Ogundele

Immigrants are not one group. We compared everyday discrimination and vigilance across Hispanic, Black, Asian, and White foreign-born adults, and tested whether discrimination explains differences in vigilance.

Code: DaveIpsos/Racial-Discrimination

What we found · vs. foreign-born NH White

+0.31 SDdiscriminationForeign-born Black adults reported more everyday discrimination.
+0.33 SDvigilanceThey also reported more vigilance, and discrimination accounted for most of that gap (partial mediation, about 75%).
−0.19 SDdiscriminationForeign-born Hispanic adults reported less discrimination, with similar vigilance.

    Group project · IDEA report

    Labor outcomes by sex and migration status in Canada

    With Moustapha Touré, Camila Iciaszczyk, and Jordan Katz

    Using the 2025 Canadian Labour Force Survey, we asked whether the gender gap in employment and full-time work differs for recent immigrants, long-term immigrants, and the Canadian-born. Survey-weighted descriptives, then logistic regression with predicted probabilities from Stata's margins.

    Read the report

    Predicted probabilities

    0.87 vs 0.69full-timeRecent immigrant men had the highest probability of full-time work; recent immigrant women had the lowest.
    0.92 = 0.92employmentAmong long-term immigrants, men and women had nearly identical employment probabilities.
    0.94 vs 0.92employmentAmong the Canadian-born, women's employment probability was slightly higher than men's.

    Policy levers: language training for recent immigrant women, subsidized childcare, and faster foreign-credential recognition.

    Final presentation

    Canada's crime rate is associated with US mortality: A spurious correlation?

    CAnD3 final presentation

    A deliberately provocative exercise in causal reasoning. Two unrelated national time series move together strongly, which is exactly why correlation alone cannot carry a causal claim. The point is to show why a DAG and a theory of the mechanism come first.

    View slides

    The correlations

    r = −0.67p = 0.0001Canada's crime rate and US life expectancy.
    r = +0.67p = 0.0001Canada's crime rate and US death rates.

    Shared time trends, not a causal link, are the likely explanation.
